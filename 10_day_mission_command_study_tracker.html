1. Algebraic Expression (Day 1)

Definition: A combination of constants and variables connected by fundamental mathematical operations ($+$, $-$, $\times$, $\div$) is called an Algebraic Expression.

Example: 4x + 7, 3a² - 5b + 9

2. Constants & Variables (Day 1)

Constant: A symbol having a fixed numerical value is called a constant.
Example: 5, -12, 7/3, π

Variable: A symbol (usually a letter) which can take various numerical values is called a variable.
Example: x, y, z, a, b

3. Terms: Like Terms & Unlike Terms (Day 1)

Terms: Several parts of an algebraic expression separated by $+$ or $-$ signs are called its terms.

Like Terms: Terms having the exact same literal factors (variables with same exponents) are called Like Terms.
Example: 5x²y and -9x²y

Unlike Terms: Terms having different literal factors (variables) are called Unlike Terms.
Example: 4xy and 7x²y

4. Types of Algebraic Expressions (Day 2)

  • • Monomial: An algebraic expression containing only one term.
    Example: 7x, -5ab², 9
  • • Binomial: An algebraic expression containing two unlike terms.
    Example: 3x + 5, a² - b²
  • • Trinomial: An algebraic expression containing three unlike terms.
    Example: x² + 5x + 6, 2a + 3b - 4c
  • • Quadrinomial: An algebraic expression containing four unlike terms.
    Example: x³ + 3x²y + 3xy² + y³

5. Chapter 5 & 6 Important Formulas (Day 6)

  • 1. aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ (Product Law of Exponents)
  • 2. aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (Quotient Law of Exponents)
  • 3. (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐˣⁿ (Power of a Power Law)
  • 4. a⁰ = 1 (Zero Exponent Identity)
  • 5. (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
  • 6. (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
  • 7. a² - b² = (a + b)(a - b)

WILLIAM SHAKESPEARE – BIOGRAPHY (Day 3 & 4)

  • • Full Name: William Shakespeare
  • • Born: 23 April 1564, Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, England
  • • Known As: "The Bard of Avon" and the National Poet of England
  • • Parents: John Shakespeare and Mary Arden
  • • Spouse: Anne Hathaway (Married in 1582)
  • • Literary Works: He wrote 39 plays, 154 sonnets, and several narrative poems.
  • • Famous Tragedies & Plays: Hamlet, Macbeth, Romeo and Juliet, Othello, Julius Caesar, The Merchant of Venice.
  • • Legacy: Widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's pre-eminent dramatist.
  • • Died: 23 April 1616 (aged 52) at Stratford-upon-Avon.

FAMOUS SHORT POEM FOR CHART PAPER (Day 5)

Option 1: "Under the Greenwood Tree" (Short, sweet & easy to write on chart paper)

Under the greenwood tree

Who loves to lie with me,

And turn his merry note

Unto the sweet bird's throat,

Come hither, come hither, come hither:

Here shall he see

No enemy

But winter and rough weather.

— William Shakespeare (As You Like It)
